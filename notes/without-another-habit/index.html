Product note

The best productivity tool is the one that does not become another job.

Modern users are not short on software. They are short on attention. DayLog should win by giving people memory and context without asking them to manage yet another system.

Tools have a cognitive price.

A tool can be useful and still fail because it adds too much overhead. Every new workflow asks the user to remember it, feed it, organize it, review it, and respond to it.

For high-agency users, the question is often not “Can this help me?” but “Is the help worth the mental load?”

Near-zero effort changes the ROI.

If a tool requires daily review, manual capture, constant approval, or another inbox, then it must deliver a lot of value to justify itself. But if the daily cost is close to zero, even small moments of later usefulness become high return.

When the cost is almost zero, every recovered idea, meeting detail, or decision context feels like free leverage.

DayLog should be useful when you need it, quiet when you do not.

The product should help when you want to reconstruct what happened, review your day, search for a forgotten detail, or turn messy life context into a journal. It should not constantly demand replies, approvals, or maintenance.

What this means for the product.

  • Default to automatic capture and organization.
  • Ask fewer questions unless a question clearly improves the outcome.
  • Make review optional, not mandatory.
  • Prefer useful summaries and searchable context over another dashboard.
