DayLog/Product notes

Concept

A memory layer that works without adding another habit.

DayLog is for people who already have too many tools. It is not trying to become another dashboard, inbox, or ritual. The goal is simpler and more ambitious: turn everyday context into searchable private memory with as little ongoing effort as possible.

The problem is not a lack of tools.

Productive people already have notes apps, task managers, calendars, meeting tools, journals, and AI chat. The hidden cost is that every tool asks for attention. You have to remember to open it, decide what belongs inside it, maintain it, and return to it later.

That means the real adoption question is not only “Is this useful?” It is “Does this add another thing I have to manage?”

DayLog should feel like extra memory, not extra work.

The product principle: start once, keep living.

The ideal interaction is close to zero. Turn DayLog on, place the device on your desk or carry it with you, and continue your day. In the background it can capture context, transcribe on device where possible, and organize useful fragments into memory.

The value appears later: when you want to reconstruct a meeting, remember why you made a decision, draft a journal entry, recover a passing idea, or search for a detail that would normally be gone.

Why “DayLog” still means life context.

DayLog is not about preserving only one day. It is about building life context day by day. Days are the natural unit of human review, reflection, and memory, but the ambition is a long-running private memory layer.

What it should become.

  • Automatic enough that you do not need a new habit.
  • Private enough that raw audio is temporary and transcription can happen on device.
  • Useful enough that small future recall benefits are worth it because daily effort is nearly zero.
  • Searchable enough that your own life context becomes easier to revisit than scattered notes.
